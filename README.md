# ARABLE-PRODUCTION-ON-CEREALS-IN-IRELAND
Abstract

Arable cereal production constitutes a significant component of the Irish agricultural sector, with barley, wheat, and oats representing the principal cereal crops cultivated across the country. This study examined the patterns, productivity, and comparative performance of cereal crop production in Ireland relative to selected European Union (EU) member states. In addition, the study explored public sentiment concerning cereal crop pricing through the application of machine learning and sentiment analysis techniques.

A CRISP-DM (Cross-Industry Standard Process for Data Mining) framework was employed to guide the processes of data exploration, transformation, modelling, and evaluation in order to achieve the study objectives. Comparative statistical analyses were conducted using a range of non-parametric inferential techniques following the application of the Shapiro–Wilk normality test, which indicated that the Irish dataset did not conform to a normal distribution. Consequently, non-parametric methods, including the Kruskal–Wallis H test, Friedman test, Wilcoxon signed-rank test, Mann–Whitney U test, and Chi-square test, were utilised to evaluate similarities and differences between Ireland and selected EU countries.

The findings revealed that Ireland’s cereal production remains comparatively modest within the European Union. France and Germany emerged as the dominant cereal-producing countries, followed by Italy and the United Kingdom. Furthermore, Ireland’s average cereal yield was estimated at 4.6 tonnes per hectare, which is below the EU average yield of 6.2 tonnes per hectare. Despite this comparatively lower output, Irish cereal crops continue to maintain a strong reputation for quality in both domestic and international markets.

To further investigate market perceptions, sentiment analysis was conducted on wheat cereal price data to classify positive and negative sentiments. The analysis indicated that negative sentiments were more prevalent than positive sentiments. Two supervised machine learning algorithms, namely Logistic Regression and Decision Tree Classifier models, were implemented to evaluate predictive performance. Hyperparameter optimisation was achieved through GridSearchCV, while model robustness was assessed using cross-validation techniques. The Decision Tree Classifier demonstrated exceptional predictive capability, achieving accuracy, precision, and recall scores of 100%, alongside a cross-validation mean score of 0.99 with a standard deviation of 0.02, indicating high model stability and minimal susceptibility to performance fluctuations. Similarly, the Logistic Regression model achieved a training accuracy of 98% and a testing accuracy of 100%, with precision, recall, and F1-scores also reaching 100%. These findings suggest that both machine learning models effectively learned from the dataset and possess strong predictive potential for sentiment classification tasks within the cereal production domain.
